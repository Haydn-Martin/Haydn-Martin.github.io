# Reality's Gravity

There is a type of curve you see everywhere. That has deep implications in
maths and science and life. The arch that limits.

I like to think of it as Reality's Gravity. It's the force that pulls us down,
that challenges us to think of new ways to make progress.

![Reality's Gravity](/images/curve.png)

## Mathematics

You see this shape everywhere.

Logarithms, useful for all sorts of mathematical reasons, look like this.

![Logarithms](/images/logarithm_plots.png)
_Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithm_

Limits, which we need for calculus, rely on this type of horizontal asymptote.

In statistics, limits and convergence are paramount. Most Big Important Statistical Laws - 
the Central Limit Theorem, the Law of Large Numbers, Maximum Likelihood Estimation, etc. -
rely on the concept of limits.

![Law of Large Numbers](/images/law_large_numbers.png)

## From Theory to Practice

That which is theorised in [statistics](https://haydn-martin.github.io/2024/08/02/The-Only-Statistics-You-Need.html) 
is reflected in [practical data science](https://haydn-martin.github.io/2025/10/24/We-Still-Don't-Know-What-a-Data-Scientist-Is.html).

![Llama Training](/images/llama_training_curves.png)
_Source: https://finbarr.ca/llms-not-trained-enough/_

In machine learning we see this curve, or its inverse, everywhere.

![Accuracy](/images/accuracy.png)
_Source: https://www.baeldung.com/cs/learning-curve-ml_

Which leads to the following concept we also see everywhere: diminishing marginal returns.
As you move from left to right, yes sure you do increase in your y-axis value, but
the _rate_ at which you do so _decreases_, such that, at the _limit_, you hit a horizontal
_asymptote_, at which point your growth rate AKA the gains in y = 0.

This is a very important concept in science, economics, and life.

![Happiness](/images/happiness_diminishing_returns.jpg)
_Source: https://www.economicshelp.org/microessays/costs/diminishing-returns/_

In [economics](https://haydn-martin.github.io/2026/06/13/Where-Are-My-Sardines.html),
we see it everywhere, from diminishing marginal utility to tax rates.

![Yield Curve](/images/yield_curve.png)
_Source: https://www.investopedia.com/terms/y/yieldcurve.asp_

In biology, from photosynthesis to evolution.

![Photosynthesis](/images/photosynthesis.png)
_Source: https://www.sansi.com/blog/how-light-intensity-influence-photosynthesis-rate_

Diminishing marginal gains naturally leads to the concept of effective use of resources.
If I have two variables that affect my y with curves like this, medium-sized gains in
both - as opposed to a large gain in one - will lead to larger changes in y. This is sort 
of Jenson's Inequality in action.

![Jenson's Inequality](/images/jensons_inequality.png)
_Source: https://seunghan96.github.io/stat/9.-em-EM_algorithm%281%29/_

So if you're trying to make progress, you better make sure you know if
you're being affected by Reality's Gravity, or not.
